## Bitget Haemorrhages $463 Million in Customer Outflows After $388 Million Theft
{: #bitget-haemorrhages-463-million-in-customer-outflows-after-388-million-theft}

[`bloomberg_crypto`](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/crypto-hack-spurs-463-million-in-customer-outflows-at-bitget) [`cryptobriefing`](https://cryptobriefing.com/bitget-ceo-skeptical-recovering-stolen-funds/) [`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/09/29/usd50-million-in-bitget-hacker-swaps-puts-near-intents-permissionless-claim-to-the-test) [`bloomberg_crypto`](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/bitcoin-pioneer-adam-back-s-empire-beset-by-hack-lawsuits)
{: .sources}

**What happened.** Bitget suffered a $388 million theft last week and has now seen roughly $463 million in net customer outflows in the 24 hours through Tuesday, as depositors voted with their wallets. CEO Gracy Chen has publicly said she is sceptical the stolen funds will be recovered.

**Why it matters.** That is the classic confidence-spiral pattern: a hack triggers withdrawals, withdrawals trigger liquidity pressure, and liquidity pressure raises the odds of the exchange being unable to honour obligations. Near Intents separately froze $50 million in swaps linked to the hacker, putting its permissionless credentials under awkward scrutiny.

**The catch.** Adam Back's Blockstream empire is reportedly dealing with its own hack alongside ongoing litigation, widening the blast radius of this week's security news. On the periphery, SEAL logged 67 incidents across the past week, a reminder that the Bitget headline is sitting on top of a sustained baseline of exploitation that does not make the front page.
