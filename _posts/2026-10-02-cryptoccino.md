## SEC Puts a Compliance Path on Paper for Crypto Custody, Finally.
{: #sec-puts-a-compliance-path-on-paper-for-crypto-custody-finally}

[`sec_press`](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-100-sec-proposal-would-address-how-investment-advisers-funds-can-custody-crypto-assets-under-federal) [`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/01/u-s-sec-maps-out-crypto-custody-in-new-proposal-that-furthers-its-digital-assets-agenda) [`theblock`](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-01-sec-proposes-crypto-custody-rule-investment-advisers-funds-417498) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/379899/sec-proposes-rules-advisers-funds-hold-crypto) [`cointelegraph_regulation`](https://cointelegraph.com/news/sec-moves-to-clear-custody-hurdle-for-advisers-offering-crypto)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">72<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Greed</span> <span class="fng-delta up">+1 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** The SEC released a proposed rulemaking that would establish a tailored custody framework for registered investment advisers and regulated funds holding crypto assets. The proposal permits self-custody under defined conditions and explicitly allows state-chartered trust companies to serve as qualified custodians, replacing years of staff guidance with actual rulemaking.

**Why it matters.** Custody ambiguity has been the single most cited reason advisers avoid offering direct crypto exposure to clients, and this proposal removes the regulatory excuse. If finalised broadly as drafted, it opens the door for a significant new tranche of institutional AUM to enter the market via compliant structures, not just ETF wrappers.

**The catch.** This is a proposal, not a final rule, and the comment period will draw heavy fire from both custody banks lobbying to exclude self-custody and DeFi participants arguing the definitions are too narrow. The CLARITY Act failed in Congress, and the SEC's own history suggests the gap between proposal and final rule can stretch well beyond a year.
