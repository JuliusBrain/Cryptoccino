## Bitcoin Catches a Double Hit as Fed Hikes and Clarity Act Dies on the Same Day.
{: #bitcoin-catches-a-double-hit-as-fed-hikes-and-clarity-act-dies-on-the-same-day}

[`bloomberg_crypto`](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/bitcoin-faces-double-whammy-after-bill-rejection-rate-hike) [`theblock`](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-09-16-bitcoin-ether-swing-unanimous-quarter-point-fed-rate-hike-warsh-aim-inflation-415307) [`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/16/fed-raises-rates-by-25-basis-points-in-first-hike-since-july-2023) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/378417/fed-hikes-rates-first-time-since-2023-bitcoin) [`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/16/bitcoin-etfs-shed-usd450-million-as-clarity-act-fails) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/378382/bitcoin-etfs-clarity-act-vote)

**What happened.** The Federal Reserve delivered a unanimous 25-basis-point hike on Wednesday, its first since July 2023, with Chair Kevin Warsh signalling inflation remains the priority regardless of political pressure. Hours earlier, the Senate failed to advance the Clarity Act, with every Democrat voting against cloture. Bitcoin and ether whipsawed on the combined news before settling around $76,000, while spot Bitcoin ETFs shed roughly $450–590 million across Tuesday and Wednesday, their worst stretch since June.

**Why it matters.** Crypto had been pricing in two potential tailwinds simultaneously: a legislative framework and a looser rate environment. Both reversed in the same session. The Fear and Greed Index sits at 50 today after dropping 19 points over the past week, placing sentiment squarely in neutral territory even as the macro setup turns more hostile. Goldman Sachs compounded the pressure overnight, revising its forecast to include a further hike in October.

**The catch.** Bitcoin's Coinbase premium has fallen to a one-month low, suggesting US-based spot demand is fading relative to offshore venues. Some analysts argue that dollar liquidity, not legislative calendars, ultimately sets the floor from here, which would make October's Fed meeting the more consequential date. The bill is not formally dead, with lame-duck session advocates already circulating, but the window for any market-moving legislative relief this year is very narrow.
