## Ledger's Hardware Supply Chain Burns $86 Million as Tampered Reseller Devices Hit Southeast Asia
{: #ledgers-hardware-supply-chain-burns-86-million-as-tampered-reseller-devices-hit-southeast-asia}

[`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/09/ledger-investigates-potential-wallet-tampering-after-reports-of-usd86-million-in-crypto-stolen) [`theblock`](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-09-ledger-cryptobilis-fund-losses-418163) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/380582/ledger-probes-87m-theft-crypto-wallet-reseller) [`bloomberg_crypto`](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/crypto-hardware-maker-ledger-says-wallets-drained-in-latest-attack) [`cryptobriefing`](https://cryptobriefing.com/ledger-theft-funds-usdt-usdd-freeze/)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">64<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Greed</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−3 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** Ledger is investigating reports that users who purchased its hardware wallets through Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis have lost funds totalling more than $86 million. Ledger has asked CryptoBilis to halt sales and warned recent buyers not to initialise their devices. An onchain investigator corroborated the scale of suspected thefts, and stolen funds are already moving from USDT to USDD to sidestep Tether freeze mechanisms.

**Why it matters.** Hardware wallet supply-chain attacks are rare but catastrophic when they occur, because victims have reasonable grounds to believe their keys never touched an internet-connected device. If devices were pre-seeded with compromised seed phrases or backdoored firmware at the reseller level, it undermines the foundational security promise of cold storage. The incident lands as broader sentiment sits at a 64 Greed reading, masking real structural risk accumulating at the custody layer.

**The catch.** Ledger has not yet confirmed the attack vector: tampered firmware, pre-generated seed phrases included in packaging, or something else entirely. Until the investigation concludes, affected users face a difficult position, as moving funds to software wallets generated on potentially compromised devices may compound rather than fix the exposure. Anyone who bought a Ledger product through a third-party reseller in the region should treat the device as untrusted and generate fresh keys on independently verified hardware.
