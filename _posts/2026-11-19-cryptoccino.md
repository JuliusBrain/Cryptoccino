**What happened.** Bitcoin fell below $63,000, logging its sixth consecutive weekly loss, after Fed Governor Waller called for a 50-basis-point rate hike in December and ETF outflows accelerated. CryptoQuant confirmed the asset has slipped below both its 200-day and 365-day moving averages, formally reopening a bearish phase. Analysts attribute the pressure to a combination of tightening macro conditions, basis-trade unwinds, and confirmed heavy selling by MARA and Riot, who offloaded a combined 33,000-plus BTC this year to fund AI data-centre buildouts.

**Why it matters.** The Anthropic IPO pricing at $83 per share and valuing the company near $100 billion grabbed the narrative, but most analysts say that is a convenient scapegoat. The more load-bearing explanation is structural: a Fed that is re-accelerating rather than pivoting, miners liquidating reserves at scale, and a spot market thin enough that the outflows register hard. With the Fear and Greed Index sitting at 10, Extreme Fear and 18 points lower than a week ago, the market is not panicking into this dip so much as it is grinding through it with no obvious catalyst to reverse.

**The catch.** K33 and CryptoQuant both flag this as a potential value zone, and options positioning for a local bottom is building. But Waller's 50bp call has not been priced fully by rates markets yet, and if December delivers that hike, the reflexive bid from risk-asset buyers will be harder to find. The miners who have already sold are gone as a source of supply pressure, which is marginally constructive, but that relief is small against the macro backdrop.
