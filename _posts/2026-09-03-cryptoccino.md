**What happened.** U.S. forces struck roughly 60 Iranian targets and escorted 40 vessels through the Strait of Hormuz, sending WTI crude above $93 and triggering a broad risk selloff. Bitcoin dipped below $76,500, SOL, ETH, and XRP led the declines, and gold also slid as the dollar firmed. By early morning Bitcoin had clawed back above $77,500, with XRP outperforming peers as Fed rate-hike odds recalibrated to around 62%.

**Why it matters.** The episode exposes Bitcoin's persistent correlation to macro risk events: the asset that was supposedly holding up against rising yields cracked the moment a genuine geopolitical shock landed. Separately, the Fear &amp; Greed Index sits at 65 (Greed) but has shed six points over seven days, suggesting the market was already losing conviction before the Iran news hit.

**Watch.** Reports emerged that Trump is in talks with Iranian counterparts to formally declare an end to hostilities, which could deflate the oil premium quickly if confirmed. A firm dollar remains the structural headwind: so long as DXY stays elevated, Bitcoin's upside is capped even if the geopolitical noise fades. The Fed's next policy decision, now priced as a genuine live meeting, is the next hard data point.
