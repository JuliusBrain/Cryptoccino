## Compromised Ledger Reseller Used to Drain Up to $93 Million From Hardware Wallet Users.
{: #compromised-ledger-reseller-used-to-drain-up-to-93-million-from-hardware-wallet-users}

[`cryptobriefing`](https://cryptobriefing.com/ledger-reseller-supply-chain-hack-implants/)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">69<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Greed</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−1 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** Hackers reportedly siphoned as much as $93 million by compromising a Ledger hardware wallet reseller, using the access point to implant malicious firmware or intercept seed phrases before devices reached end users. The attack vector appears to be the supply chain itself, not Ledger's own infrastructure or the Ledger Connect Kit.

**Why it matters.** Supply-chain attacks on hardware wallet resellers are among the hardest threats for retail and institutional holders to defend against, because the device looks and functions normally until the drain happens. At $93 million, this is one of the largest cold-wallet-adjacent losses on record, and it will force procurement teams to revisit vendor verification across the board. The Fear &amp; Greed Index sits at 69 (Greed) despite this, suggesting most of the market hasn't priced the reputational drag yet.

**The catch.** Figures circulating are unverified; Ledger has not issued a formal post-mortem as of filing time, so the $93 million ceiling may shift. What is already clear: anyone who purchased a Ledger device through a third-party reseller and has not verified firmware authenticity should treat their current wallet as potentially compromised and rotate to a freshly sourced device before moving funds.
