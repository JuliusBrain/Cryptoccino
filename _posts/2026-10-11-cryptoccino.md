## One Year After the $19 Billion Wipeout, Crypto's Structural Scars Are Still Visible
{: #one-year-after-the-19-billion-wipeout-cryptos-structural-scars-are-still-visible}

[`bloomberg_crypto`](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-10/one-year-after-19-billion-crash-crypto-faces-a-new-reality) [`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/10/bitcoin-s-usd19-billion-wake-up-call-one-year-later-has-crypto-learned-anything) [`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/10/one-year-after-10-10-bitcoin-and-ether-liquidity-have-rebuilt-but-other-altcoins-still-face-risks)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">61<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Greed</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−4 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** On 10 October last year, a single day of forced liquidations erased $19 billion in leveraged positions, the largest single-day deleveraging event on record. The anniversary lands as a retrospective moment for the industry, with analysts picking over what, if anything, has structurally changed in the year since.

**Why it matters.** Bitcoin and ether order-book depth has broadly recovered, but altcoin liquidity remains thin and concentrated, leaving the long tail of the market exposed to the same cascade dynamics. The Fear &amp; Greed Index sits at 61 today, Greed territory, down four points over the past week. That's a mildly uncomfortable echo of the complacency that preceded the original crash.

**The catch.** Realised volatility in BTC has compressed on a headline basis, yet the frequency of extreme daily moves has risen compared to 2018, suggesting calmer averages are masking fatter tails. The market is better capitalised and better regulated in spots, but the leverage appetite never left, it just found new venues.
