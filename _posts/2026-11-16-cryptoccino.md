## Bitcoin's dominant options bet has flipped to downside, with the $60K put carrying nearly $900M in open interest.
{: #bitcoins-dominant-options-bet-has-flipped-to-downside-with-the-60k-put-carrying-nearly-900m-in-open-interest}

[`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/11/16/bitcoin-s-biggest-bet-is-now-a-drop-below-usd60-000-not-a-rally-above-usd100-000) [`cryptobriefing`](https://cryptobriefing.com/bitcoin-60k-put-open-interest-deribit/) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/383282/bitcoin-etfs-lose-2-1-billion-november-outflow-streak-seven-days) [`cryptobriefing`](https://cryptobriefing.com/bitcoin-price-volatility-below-62000-recovery/)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">10<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Extreme Fear</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−19 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** Bitcoin slid below $62,000 for the first time since July before clawing back to around $65,000. On Deribit, the $60K put has accumulated roughly $900M in open interest, now the single largest options position in the market. Spot Bitcoin ETFs logged a seventh consecutive day of outflows on Friday, bringing November's total redemptions to approximately $2.1B.

**Why it matters.** The options positioning represents a structural shift: the crowd is no longer paying up to bet on a breakout to $100K, it is hedging aggressively against a leg lower. That reorientation, combined with Glassnode data showing whale wallets sending a net $55M more to exchanges than they withdraw each day, points to sustained distribution rather than a brief dip. The Fear &amp; Greed Index sits at 10, Extreme Fear, down 19 points over the past week, so sentiment is not providing a floor here.

**The catch.** CZ publicly denied that Binance liquidated holdings into the sell-off, and Bitcoin did bounce, so the move is not a one-way rout. Short-term holder whales are sitting on a record $8B in unrealised losses, which creates both forced-seller risk and a potential capitulation floor if that cohort fully flushes. The regime only changes if ETF flows reverse or spot demand reappears at scale.
