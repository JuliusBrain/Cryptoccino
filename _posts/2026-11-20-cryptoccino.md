## Bitcoin prints a death cross at a four-month low as Fed rate-hike bets and an AI rotation drain spot ETFs of nearly $3 billion in November.
{: #bitcoin-prints-a-death-cross-at-a-four-month-low-as-fed-rate-hike-bets-and-an-ai-rotation-drain-spot-etfs-of-nearly-3-billion-in-november}

**What happened.** Bitcoin slid to its lowest level since July, briefly touching the low $62,000s, as the Anthropic IPO sucked capital into AI names and Federal Reserve officials stoked rate-hike expectations. The 50-day moving average crossed below the 200-day on the daily chart, completing a death cross. Spot ETFs have now seen roughly $2.9 billion in net outflows through November, with momentum indicators deeply oversold and near-term support looking thin.

**Why it matters.** This is the sixth consecutive weekly decline for bitcoin, an unusual streak that signals sustained institutional de-risking rather than a routine pullback. The Fear &amp; Greed Index sits at 12 (Extreme Fear) with a seven-point drop over seven days, yet institutions were net buyers of $4 billion in bitcoin funds as recently as Q3, so the structural bid has not vanished, it has simply stepped back. A prolonged rate-hike cycle would reprice risk assets broadly and test whether that Q3 accumulation holds.

**The catch.** CryptoQuant analysts describe current levels as a 'value zone', and Sygnum argues bitcoin carry trades could outperform Treasury bills in 2027 as funding rates recover. The bullish structural case depends entirely on the Fed pivot timeline, which is now being pushed out by analysts citing backward-looking data on gas prices and labour. Until rate expectations stabilise, technicals dominate.
