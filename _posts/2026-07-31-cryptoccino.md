## Coldcard Mk3 Users Told to Move Funds Immediately as 594 BTC Theft Reports Pile Up
{: #coldcard-mk3-users-told-to-move-funds-immediately-as-594-btc-theft-reports-pile-up}

[`theblock`](https://www.theblock.co/post/410235/coinkite-coldcard-mk3-warning?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss) [`cryptobriefing`](https://cryptobriefing.com/coinkite-coldcard-mk3-firmware-rng-security-warning/)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">25<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Extreme Fear</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−3 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** Coinkite issued an emergency advisory urging all Coldcard Mk3 owners to generate a strong BIP-39 passphrase directly on the device and migrate funds to the resulting wallet. The warning follows reports of theft totalling 594 BTC, with the manufacturer pointing to a compromised random-number generator in the older hardware as the likely attack surface.

**Why it matters.** Hardware wallets sit at the end of the security chain. If the RNG is weak, every key generated on that device is potentially predictable, meaning funds could be swept without any phishing, malware, or physical access. The Fear &amp; Greed Index is sitting at 25, deep into extreme fear territory, and a hardware-wallet scare at this moment risks accelerating outflows from self-custody back into exchange custody — the opposite of where the industry wants retail to go.

**The catch.** Coinkite has not confirmed the root cause publicly or released a CVE, and the 594 BTC figure comes from user reports rather than a verified post-mortem. Until the attack path is pinned down, it is not clear whether the passphrase mitigation fully closes the exposure or just reduces it. Mk4 and Q-series owners appear unaffected, but anyone who generated keys on an Mk3 and later migrated the same seed to newer hardware may still be at risk.
