## Bitcoin breaks below $80,000 as US Middle East evacuations trigger a rapid $404 million long flush.
{: #bitcoin-breaks-below-80000-as-us-middle-east-evacuations-trigger-a-rapid-404-million-long-flush}

[`theblock`](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-10-24-crypto-longs-lose-404-million-in-four-hours-as-bitcoin-briefly-slips-below-80000-431608) [`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/24/crypto-under-weekend-pressure-bitcoin-slides-below-usd80-000-on-u-s-middle-east-evacuations)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">54<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Neutral</span> <span class="fng-delta up">+1 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** Reports of US personnel evacuations from the Middle East hit over the weekend, sending bitcoin through the $80,000 floor and triggering roughly $404 million in long liquidations across crypto markets in four hours. Spot bitcoin ETFs had already shed $773 million across Thursday and Friday, snapping three days of net inflows, per SoSoValue data.

**Why it matters.** The move confirms that bitcoin's correlation with macro risk sentiment remains intact under stress, regardless of the longer-term reserve-asset narrative. The Fear and Greed Index sitting at 54, neutral and barely changed on the week, suggests the broader participant base is not yet treating this as a regime break, but that reading can reprice quickly if evacuations escalate into something larger.

**The catch.** Houthi missile activity escalated sharply over the same window, with intercepts reported over Mecca, Medina and Riyadh and the group threatening continued strikes on Saudi airports. The geopolitical risk premium is not priced in steadily, it arrives in sharp jolts, and perpetual funding rates are now reset to levels that punish the next attempt to rebuild longs in size.
