## Binance Loses $6 Billion in History's Largest Exchange Hack, Lazarus Group Suspected.
{: #binance-loses-6-billion-in-historys-largest-exchange-hack-lazarus-group-suspected}

**What happened.** Binance suffered an estimated $5.5 to $6.3 billion outflow from hot wallets across multiple chains in what blockchain researchers are calling the largest exchange hack on record. Tether and Circle moved quickly to freeze roughly $1.79 billion in USDT and USDC tied to the attacker, while BitGo separately froze a USD1 address linked to the stolen funds. Binance co-CEO Yi He confirmed the exchange will cover all user losses; an early internal assessment points to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop as the likely entry point.

**Why it matters.** Reserve data shows the drained wallets were the same ones backing customer assets, meaning proof-of-reserves figures published before the incident were materially overstated at the moment users needed them most. Institutional players read the situation immediately: on-chain data shows a broad flight of funds off centralised exchanges within hours of the news breaking, the kind of reflexive withdrawal cascade that historically takes weeks to fully unwind. Standard Chartered flagged further near-term price pressure, and with the Fear and Greed Index already at 30 before the hack landed, sentiment has little cushion left.

**The catch.** Blockchain sleuths, pointing to wallet clustering and laundering patterns, attribute the attack to North Korea's Lazarus Group, though attribution at this scale is rarely clean. The hacker has already split roughly $1.4 billion of bitcoin across 19 wallets, converting freezable stablecoins into ETH on decentralised venues to reduce the attack surface for future freezes. Sanctioned funds at this size have historically taken years to move without triggering detection, but $4-plus billion in non-freezable assets is a very long runway.
