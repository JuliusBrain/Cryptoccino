## Coldcard Exploit Crosses $114 Million as Fourth Sweep Emerges and Self-Custody Confidence Cracks

**What happened.** The Coldcard hardware wallet exploit has entered its fifth day, with Galaxy Research flagging a probable fourth wave of fund sweeps pushing estimated losses toward $114 million. On-chain data shows transfers of under 1 BTC spiking to levels last seen in the days after the FTX collapse, as affected holders scramble to move remaining funds. Bitcoin slipped below $63,000 on the news, with Ether following.

**Why it matters.** This is no longer a contained incident. The persistence of fresh sweeps suggests the attacker either has access to a broader key set than initially understood, or is methodically working through a list. Jameson Lopp has framed it as a stress test of Bitcoin's 'don't trust, verify' ethos, noting that AI-assisted bug discovery is giving attackers a structural edge over the pace of community audits. The Fear and Greed Index sitting at 25, Extreme Fear, with a further four-point slide over the week, reflects a market that was already fragile before this news compounded it.

**The catch.** The attack vector has not been publicly confirmed at the protocol level, which means affected users cannot yet assess their own exposure with precision. Air-gapped wallet holders who believed they were immune are reassessing, and the conversation about whether hardware wallet firmware signing offers adequate guarantees is now very much open.
