## Bitcoin Loses Its Liquidity Floor as Stablecoins Drain $14 Billion and ETF Outflows Hit Four Consecutive Days.

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. **What happened.** Stablecoin supply contracted $14.2 billion over the past 30 days, the sharpest drawdown since June 2022, with USDT and USDC accounting for nearly $11 billion of that fall. Bitcoin ETFs have now shed assets for four straight days, with BlackRock's IBIT alone logging $269.7 million in outflows. BTC is trading below $74,000 after five consecutive weekly losses, while options markets are pricing a 31% probability of a sub-$70,000 print by Christmas.

**Why it matters.** Stablecoin supply is the nearest-term proxy for dry powder sitting on the sidelines, and a contraction of this magnitude signals capital is leaving the ecosystem rather than rotating. The Fear and Greed Index sits at 28, down 22 points over the past week, so sentiment is deteriorating fast rather than stabilising. Short-term holders sent $3.6 billion in BTC to exchanges at a loss this week, the kind of capitulation signal that precedes either a floor or a flush.

**The catch.** Bitcoin barely moved on a hotter-than-expected CPI print, which could read as macro desensitisation or simply as an asset with no buyers left to react. The same session saw AI stocks rebound sharply on CoreWeave earnings, so the divergence between crypto and risk-on equities is now explicit. Until ETF flows reverse and stablecoin supply stabilises, every bounce looks like a short-squeeze candidate rather than a regime change.
