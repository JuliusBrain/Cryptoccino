## Cronos Shuts Down the Entire Chain After $75 Million Tectonic Exploit
{: #cronos-shuts-down-the-entire-chain-after-75-million-tectonic-exploit}

[`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/08/31/cronos-halts-blockchain-after-usd75-million-lending-exploit-hits-lending-app-tectonic) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/376913/crypto-coms-cronos-halts-entire-blockchain-after-75m-tectonic-exploit) [`bloomberg_crypto`](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/crypto-com-linked-lending-platform-hit-by-74-million-exploit)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">69<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Greed</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−5 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** An attacker manipulated token collateral prices on Tectonic, the Crypto.com-linked lending protocol on Cronos, borrowing and draining roughly $75 million. Validators halted the entire Cronos chain after approximately $6 million made it to Ethereum, freezing the remainder on-chain.

**Why it matters.** Halting a live L1 to contain an exploit is an extraordinary step, and one that cuts both ways: it stopped further bleed but demonstrated that the chain's decentralisation claims have a hard ceiling. The Fear &amp; Greed Index sits at 69 despite the incident, suggesting the broader market is treating this as chain-specific rather than systemic, though that complacency has burned people before.

**The catch.** The chain had still not resumed block production at time of writing, leaving all Cronos users and DeFi positions in limbo. Separately, More Markets on Flow suffered a $9.3 million drain via a similar overborrowing vector using an Ankr liquid staking token, suggesting the collateral-manipulation playbook is making the rounds this cycle.
