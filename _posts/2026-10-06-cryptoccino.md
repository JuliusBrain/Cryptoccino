## Treasury Pulls Mixer and Unhosted-Wallet Rules, Calling Them a Chill on Legitimate Use
{: #treasury-pulls-mixer-and-unhosted-wallet-rules-calling-them-a-chill-on-legitimate-use}

[`cointelegraph_regulation`](https://cointelegraph.com/news/fincen-crypto-mixing-rule-legitimate-activity?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss_tag_regulation&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound) [`theblock`](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-05-fincen-drops-crypto-mixing-rule-self-hosted-wallet-proposal-417690) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/380120/treasury-kills-crypto-unhosted-wallet-mixer-surveillance-rules) [`coincenter`](https://coincenter.org/treasury-just-withdrew-crypto-surveillance-rule-proposals-long-opposed-by-coin-center/)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">73<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Greed</span> <span class="fng-delta flat">±0 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** FinCEN formally withdrew two long-contested proposals: a 2020 plan that would have imposed reporting requirements on transactions involving self-custodial wallets, and a 2023 proposal to designate crypto mixing as a primary money laundering concern under the PATRIOT Act. The withdrawals were framed explicitly as part of the Trump administration's deregulatory agenda.

**Why it matters.** Both rules had been treated by privacy advocates and self-custody defenders as existential threats to non-custodial infrastructure. Killing them removes a legal cloud that had hung over mixer operators, wallet developers, and anyone building self-sovereign tooling for years. The Fear &amp; Greed Index sits at 73 and flat on the week, suggesting the market had already priced in a friendly regulatory posture rather than treating this as a surprise catalyst.

**The catch.** Withdrawal is not the same as a statutory prohibition on future rulemaking. A later administration could re-file under the same PATRIOT Act authority with different framing, and the underlying surveillance apparatus remains intact. The spot-market regulatory gap also persists, as the CFTC's simultaneous proposal this week (see On the Hill) covers leveraged retail trading but leaves unregulated spot venues in a grey zone.
