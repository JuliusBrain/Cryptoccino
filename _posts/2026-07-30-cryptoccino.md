## An AI model weakened a post-quantum algorithm Bitcoin's migration plan assumed would hold.
{: #an-ai-model-weakened-a-post-quantum-algorithm-bitcoins-migration-plan-assumed-would-hold}

[`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/07/29/bitcoin-s-quantum-plan-assumes-some-algorithms-break-ai-just-weakened-one-in-60-hours) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/374651/new-research-bitcoin-wallet-quantum-attacks-safe) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/374637/morning-minute-claude-mythos-breaks-post-quantum-cryptography)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">28<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Fear</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−3 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** Claude Mythos, Anthropic's latest model, partially broke a lattice-based post-quantum algorithm in roughly 60 hours. Bitcoin's working quantum migration plan has been structured around the assumption that certain algorithms, including lattice-based candidates, would remain computationally hard for the foreseeable future. Separate research published in parallel outlines a compatibility layer that could let Bitcoin wallets migrate to post-quantum signatures without forcing address changes.

**Why it matters.** The quantum threat has been treated as a long-horizon problem, with most estimates placing meaningful risk a decade or more out. AI-assisted cryptanalysis compresses that runway in ways that are hard to model, because capability jumps are lumpy and fast. The Fear &amp; Greed Index sitting at 28 suggests the market is not pricing any of this urgency in, which may reflect rational discounting or simple inattention.

**The catch.** One weakened algorithm is not a broken ecosystem, and the research community distinguishes between partial attacks and full breaks. The practical exploit path from here to signing fraudulent Bitcoin transactions remains long. But the relevant question for protocol developers is whether the migration timeline needs to be pulled forward before the next capability jump arrives uninvited.
