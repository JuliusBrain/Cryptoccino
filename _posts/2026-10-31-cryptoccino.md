## North Korea's TraderTraitor drains $6.3B from Binance in the largest crypto hack on record.
{: #north-koreas-tradertraitor-drains-63b-from-binance-in-the-largest-crypto-hack-on-record}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">30<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Fear</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−21 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** Starting around 4:20 p.m. ET on 29 October, funds began leaving Binance hot wallets across multiple chains. TRM Labs and Chainalysis both attribute the attack to TraderTraitor, the North Korea-linked group responsible for the Bybit hack earlier this year. CEO Richard Teng confirmed the breach involved EIP-7702 delegation, a self-destruct mechanism, and multiple compromise points, not a single intrusion vector.

**Why it matters.** At $6.3 billion this eclipses every prior crypto theft and accounts for the bulk of North Korea-linked hack losses, which Chainalysis now puts at nearly 89% of all stolen crypto in 2026. Binance has ringfenced $6.34 billion of its own funds to cover user losses and says withdrawals will restart Sunday, but roughly $3 billion has been frozen by partners including Circle, which locked $823 million in USDC. The Fear and Greed Index sat at 30 heading into the session, and that caution proved warranted: the hack landed on an already-fragile tape.

**The catch.** Experts are split on whether the frozen assets can actually be recovered, and roughly 120 million TRX has already moved to an unknown wallet beyond freezing reach. The wider breach scope Teng disclosed suggests Binance's internal forensics are still incomplete, which makes the Sunday withdrawal target feel optimistic. The FBI and Secret Service have joined the investigation, but state-sponsored attackers with established laundering infrastructure are a different problem from ordinary theft recovery.
