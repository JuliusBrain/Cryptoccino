## MSCI Finalises Rules That Could Eject Strategy and Metaplanet From Global Equity Indexes
{: #msci-finalises-rules-that-could-eject-strategy-and-metaplanet-from-global-equity-indexes}

[`coindesk`](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/16/msci-approves-rules-that-could-eject-strategy-metaplanet-from-equity-indexes) [`decrypt`](https://decrypt.co/381168/bitcoin-treasury-giant-strategy-faces-index-removal-under-finalized-msci-rules)
{: .sources}

<div class="fng-chip" aria-label="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index" title="Crypto Fear &amp; Greed Index. Scale 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed)."><span class="fng-label">Fear &amp; Greed</span><span class="fng-value">55<span class="fng-scale">/100</span></span><span class="fng-class">Greed</span> <span class="fng-delta down">−9 / 7d</span></div>

**What happened.** MSCI has approved a reclassification framework that designates firms whose balance sheets are dominated by a single non-operating asset as ineligible for inclusion. The rules take effect 30 November, putting Strategy and Japan's Metaplanet directly in the firing line given their concentrated bitcoin treasury positions.

**Why it matters.** Forced index removal triggers automatic selling by passive funds that track MSCI benchmarks, creating structural downward pressure on both stocks independent of underlying bitcoin prices. The timing is notable: the Fear and Greed Index sits at 55 but has shed nine points over the past week, suggesting the market was already softening before this institutional overhang materialised.

**The catch.** Neither firm is guaranteed ejection. MSCI's language targets companies that are predominantly non-operating, and both companies could argue operational narratives around their treasury strategies. Watch for management responses and any index committee consultation windows between now and the November cutoff.
